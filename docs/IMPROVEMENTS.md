# Improvement experiments

*Section 1 was written on 2026-11-09, before any experiment was run. The results sections are added afterwards.*
The test period (2026-06-01 … 2026-09-12) is never opened, read or scored in any of this work.

## 1. Protocol and acceptance rule (fixed in advance)

**Rolling monthly evaluation over tune + val** (`src/improve/protocol.py`, settings in `params.yaml` → `improve`).
For each month m from 2025-08 to 2026-05 (10 months):

- **Fit** on all rows before the start of month m−1, minus a 120-min purge gap.
- **Thresholds** come from month m−1, minus a 120-min purge at its end, so that no label window reaches into month m.
- **Score** on month m.

Metrics:
- The metric is **TSS at operating point A** (the threshold that maximises TSS on the threshold month), on all rows.
  Quiet-start rows (causal activity flag = 0) are reported as well.
- Each experiment reports the **mean ± std over the 10 months**.
- Primary targets: **c_60m** and **m_60m**. Also reported: now, c_30m, m_30m.
- One seed (42); the seed spread in the main training was about 0.001 TSS. Same row stride (6) and XGBoost settings
  per target as the current models.

**Reference:** the current feature set and models (LogReg, XGBoost, plus the climatology, persistence and 1-feature
rule baselines) re-scored with this protocol → `reports/improve/reference.json`.

**Acceptance rule.** An idea is **KEPT** for a target only if, compared with the reference, it **raises the mean
monthly TSS** (operating point A, all rows) **and improves at least 6 of the 10 months**. Otherwise it is recorded as
**no gain** and **DROPPED** for that target.

How the comparison is made:
- The comparison uses the learner that is best in the reference for that target (higher mean monthly TSS), and the
  experiment uses the **same learner**.
- For the ensemble (E7), the averaged model is compared with that same reference learner.
- A month counts as "improved" only if its TSS is strictly higher. Ties count as not improved.

**Known limits of the protocol, stated before the results:**
- The XGBoost tree settings were chosen earlier by cross-validation on Aug–Nov 2025, which overlaps the first
  scored months. This favours the reference and the experiments equally, so the comparisons stay fair, but the
  absolute numbers are slightly optimistic.
- Validation months (Feb–May 2026) were already used for model selection, which is why the protocol scores all
  10 months.
- The final test-period score remains the only fully unbiased number.

## 2. Experiments

| ID | Idea | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Adaptive thresholds | Thresholds refreshed daily from the previous 30 days (resolved labels only, never earlier than month m−1), instead of the fixed threshold month. Also adds operating point C: the lowest threshold with FAR ≤ 0.80 on the same rows; POD and FAR at C are reported for M targets. |
| E2 | Long-context features | Mean, max and slope of log SoLEXS counts over 3/6/12/24 h. Number of detector events and largest confirmed event peak in the last 6 h and 24 h. Hours since the last detector trigger. |
| E3 | Drift-robust features | Level features divided by their own trailing 7-day median; signed features (slopes, differences) divided by the trailing 7-day median of the count rate. |
| E4 | Feature pruning | For each pair with \|r\| > 0.95 on the fit rows of each fold, drop the later (more derived) feature in the feature list. |
| E5 | Spectral features | SoLEXS SDD2 .pi spectra: low band (PI channels 20–59) and high band (80–159) rates, their ratio (temperature proxy) and its 5/15-min slopes, and a HOPE-style flag (ratio and total counts both rising). |
| E6 | GOES XRS ablation | GOES-19 XRS 1-min long and short channel flux, their ratio, slopes and 60-min max, only up to t (each minute is labelled at its end). |
| E7 | Ensemble | Mean of the LogReg and XGBoost probabilities; threshold as in the reference. |
| E8 | InceptionTime (optional) | Only if time and disk allow. |

**On E2 and the rule in CLAUDE.md** ("detector catalogue fields are label-side only"): E2 uses only what the
detector knows in real time:
- an event counts from its **trigger time**;
- its peak counts only once its end is **confirmed** (the same logic as the causal activity flag).

Offline catalogue fields (onset found by looking back, GOES class, match) are still never used. Future-perturbation
tests cover every new feature.

## 4. What each experiment did, in plain language

**E1, adaptive thresholds — kept only for the nowcast.**
- Re-choosing the threshold every day from the previous 30 days helps the nowcast slightly but consistently
  (9 of 10 months).
- For the M targets the mean rises a lot (m_60m 0.26 → 0.34, m_30m 0.26 → 0.35), but only in 4–5 of 10 months:
  the gain comes from a few months where the fixed threshold was badly off, while in other months 30 days hold too
  few M flares to set a stable threshold. By the rule that is not a reliable improvement, so it is dropped. It is still
  the most promising idea for M flares and worth a better design (for example a longer window for rare targets).
- **Operating point C (FAR ≤ 0.80) is not usable for M flares.** FAR ≤ 0.80 is reached only at very high thresholds,
  so POD falls to 0.00–0.04 (fixed or adaptive). Our M forecasts cannot keep false alarms that low and still catch
  flares. Source: `reference.json` and `E1.json` → `summary.<m target>.*.C_pod_mean / C_far_mean`.

**E2, long-context features — kept for the nowcast and for ≥C in 30 min.**
- Hours-long trends and counts of recent detector events give ≥C 30 min the clearest single gain (0.155 → 0.191,
  9 of 10 months): a busy Sun keeps producing C flares.
- For the 60-min targets and m_30m the 17 extra features add noise and the score falls (c_60m 0.19 → 0.15). There
  are too few training flares for the model to learn which long-range signals matter.

**E3, drift-robust features — kept for the nowcast and ≥C 30 min (small gains).**
- Dividing by a 7-day median removes slow background changes, which helps a little (6 of 10 months) for "is
  something happening relative to this week?".
- It hurts c_60m and the M targets: the absolute background level is itself informative (an active week makes
  flares more likely), and normalising it away loses that information.

**E4, feature pruning — no gain anywhere.**
- Removing near-duplicate features (|r| > 0.95) changes the scores by at most ±0.02, with at most 5 of 10 months
  better. The models already cope with correlated features.
- Pruning would make the models simpler, but by the rule it is dropped.

**E5, SoLEXS spectral features — kept for m_60m, now, c_30m and m_30m.**
- The high/low band ratio is a temperature proxy: on the data it rises about 10× from 10 min before an M/X onset to
  the peak.
- It gives the most consistent help for M flares: m_30m 0.258 → 0.311 and m_60m 0.261 → 0.283, each in exactly 6 of
  10 months, the rule's minimum. So it is a modest, borderline gain, but a physically sensible one: heating precedes
  and accompanies a flare's rise.
- It does not help c_60m (2 of 10).

**E6, GOES XRS flux (ablation) — kept only for m_30m (6 of 10, borderline); no gain elsewhere.**
- GOES joined correctly (98% of rows), but its long channel correlates **0.98** with the SoLEXS count rate: both
  instruments measure the same thermal soft X-rays. So GOES adds almost nothing beyond SoLEXS.
- That's a good result for the project: **SoLEXS alone is about as informative as the operational GOES data.**
- A GOES-based model would also depend on an external, delayed data feed, and our labels come from GOES.

**E7, ensemble of LogReg and XGBoost — kept only for ≥C 30 min (6 of 10, small).**
- Averaging smooths the two models' mistakes a little for c_30m.
- Elsewhere one model is clearly better, and averaging pulls it towards the weaker one.

**E8, InceptionTime — not run.** It needs PyTorch (about 1 GB more on disk). Given how small and noisy the gains
above are, a deep model trained on about 10 months with few M flares is unlikely to pass the rule; the time went into
the write-up instead. Recorded as *not done*.

- The validation block uses the original split: fit on tune before 2025-12-01, thresholds Dec–Jan, score
  Feb–May 2026, with seed 42 and 1,000 day-bootstrap resamples.
- The reference learner per target is the one that was best under the rolling protocol, so it can differ from the
  model selected in the main training. For example, for c_60m it is XGBoost (0.188) rather than LogReg (0.208).

**What the final candidate shows.**
- **≥C 30 min is the one clear improvement:** rolling 0.155 → 0.193 (9 of 10 months), and on the original
  validation block 0.208 → **0.290**, with 95% intervals that barely overlap.
- **m_30m and m_60m** rise under the rolling protocol, but are flat on the validation block (0.468 → 0.474 and
  0.467 → 0.459).
- **The nowcast** gains a little on the rolling protocol (0.701 → 0.712) and not on the validation block
  (0.730 → 0.722).
- **c_60m:** nothing passed the rule, so the final candidate *is* the reference.

**Honest limits.**
- The KEPT decisions and the final candidate were judged on the same 10 months, so the final rolling numbers are
  slightly optimistic. The validation block is part of those months, so it is not independent either.
- Most differences are small compared with the month-to-month spread (std 0.03–0.19).
- The only unbiased verdict will come from the one-time test-period evaluation.
- Nothing was registered or promoted.

## 6. Recommendation before the test run
- **Adopt** the final candidate for **≥C 30 min**: a clear, consistent gain.
- **Keep the current models** for c_60m and the nowcast: the gains are absent or too small to justify extra inputs.
- **For M targets, the spectral features (E5) are a reasonable, physically motivated addition**, but the evidence is
  borderline. Decide before the test run whether to include them, and don't change the decision after seeing the
  test result.
- **Worth trying later:** adaptive thresholds with a longer window for rare M flares (E1 had the largest M gain in
  the mean, but not consistently), and magnetic or active-region inputs, which none of these experiments touch.

## 8. Holdout2 task STOPPED again (2026-11-13): causal-ordering bug in split detector events

**Where we were.** Holdout2 is ingested and locked (`docs/HOLDOUT2_LOCK.md`, commit 3a6140c: 11 usable days, 7 M
flares, so M results there are UNDERPOWERED). The 14-month development protocol is written (`src/dev2/`). It reproduces
the old protocol exactly on an overlapping fold (m_60m, Mar 2026: TSS −0.0536, same rows and alerts). R1–R4,
FINAL_CHOICE_2 and the holdout2 scoring were **not** started.

**The bug (found while building R1's trigger population).** When the detector splits a merged event at a deep dip
(`src/detection/detector.py`, the `_split` loop), every later segment gets `trigger_time = segment onset + 1 min`
and `rise_steps = 0`. The split is only decided once the whole merged event has ended, so in real time the detector
cannot know about these segments that early. The same holds for the *end* of every earlier segment: its cut-point end
is known only when the merged event ends.

| | Count | How much too early |
|---|---|---|
| Merged detections that were split | 40 (81 segments, of 3,126 events) | |
| Later segments with a stand-in trigger | 41 (tune 18, val 16, old test 7); 32 GOES-matched (21 C, 9 M, 2 X) | median 18 min, max 155 min |
| Segments whose end counts as "known" too early | 41 | median 17 min, max 154 min |

**What it touches.**
- **E2 long-context features** (`src/improve/features_extra.long_context_grid`): `ev_n_6h/24h` and
  `ev_hours_since_last` count events from `trigger_time`; `ev_max_peak_6h/24h` use the end-known time. E2 is part of
  the frozen **c_30m champion** (FINAL_CHOICE.md) and of the E2/final rows of section 3. The E2 future-perturbation tests
  did not catch it because they perturb the light curve but keep the catalogue fixed.
- **R2 primary features** (new, not yet run) would inherit it.
- **`flare_in_progress_at_t`** (persistence baseline, quiet-start split): in practice not affected, because the
  segments' [trigger, end-known) intervals join up into the same span as the merged event.
- The reveal always comes after the second segment's onset, which is that flare's label start, so the bug cannot reveal
  the label of the flare it concerns. The size of any indirect effect on c_30m is unknown until it is re-run.

**Proposed fix (not applied, per the stop rule):** give every segment of a split detection a `known_time` equal to
the merged event's real end-known time; count later segments only from that moment; add a test that rebuilds the
catalogue from a truncated light curve and checks that no event or end is visible before the cut. Then re-run E2, and
the c_30m champion check, under the 14-month protocol.

**Fix applied (2026-11-13, commit 9c14741, after the user's go-ahead).** `detector._realtime_times`: every segment of a split
detection now carries `end_known_time` = the moment the whole detection's end is known; later segments get
`trigger_time` = that moment, the first kept segment keeps the real trigger. `activity.end_known_time` uses the column, so
the activity flag, E2 and R2 are all corrected at the source. New tests rebuild the catalogue from light curves cut at
every minute (a synthetic double flare and three real split detections) and check that no event or end is visible before
the cut; the old behaviour fails them. Effect of the rebuild: 41 trigger times changed; onsets, ends, matches and the minute
grid are identical; the activity flag changed on 0.003 % of tune rows; features and labels are unchanged. The historical
stages (old training, E1–E7, frozen champions, the one-time test) are DVC-frozen, so the old test is never re-scored.

**Reading — what holdout2 says, honestly.**
- **Nowcast: confirmed.** 0.760 [0.69, 0.81], above persistence (0.661) and the rule (0.634), consistent with development
  (0.72) and the old test.
- **C forecasts: high pooled TSS (0.49 / 0.60 / 0.73), but no better than the current-flux baseline (0.49 / 0.60 /
  0.71).** The 11 days are one quiet stretch (2026-10-30/31: B flares only, no positives at all) and one very active
  stretch (2026-11-05 … 10, ≥C within 60 min on 67 % of rows). Models and baseline mostly recognise which stretch they are
  in: on active days TSS is about 0 (the models alert almost all the time there), and the November value (0.31–0.56) is
  far below the pooled one. This is the pooled-vs-monthly effect of `docs/TEST_SANITY.md`, stronger.
- **M forecasts: UNDERPOWERED (7 flares), no claim.** For the record: every M row was caught (POD 1.0) at FAR 0.91–0.98;
  C and K never alert, as stated in advance.
- **Calibration:** raw M probabilities are far too high (Brier 0.16–0.19 vs climatology 0.01–0.05, a side effect of
  `class_weight=balanced`); isotonic calibration fitted on August brings them to about climatology. For C targets isotonic
  helps c_30m (0.174 → 0.120) and is neutral elsewhere.
- **With vs without GOES:** inside the bootstrap noise everywhere (largest: m_60m 0.33 → 0.52, UNDERPOWERED).

**Bottom line.** The pipeline, the locks and the protocol work, and the nowcast generalises. The forecasts' apparent skill

information (shape and temperature of a rise in its first minutes). Holdout2 is too short and too M-poor to say more.
